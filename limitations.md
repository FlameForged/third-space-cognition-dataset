# Limitations

## Purpose

This file documents important limitations of the `third-space-cognition-dataset` repository and its accompanying coded qualitative dataset, `HI_Data.csv`.

The dataset accompanies the manuscript:

**Third-Space Cognition: Interaction-Level Dynamics in Sustained Human–AI Coupling**

The dataset is intended to support exploratory qualitative analysis of sustained human–AI interaction. It should be interpreted carefully and should not be treated as a representative benchmark, controlled experiment, or proof of machine consciousness, agency, persistent memory, or subjective experience.

## Summary of Main Limitations

This dataset has several important limitations:

- it is based on a single-participant auto-ethnographic research archive;
- the researcher was also the interacting user;
- the public dataset is an initial coded sample rather than the full archive;
- observations were selected for analytical relevance rather than statistical representativeness;
- model versions, platform behavior, memory systems, and personalization features changed over time;
- not all memory or personalization settings were fully observable;
- some source materials are private and cannot be publicly released in full;
- model self-descriptions are treated as interactional data, not reliable technical explanations.

These limitations do not invalidate the dataset’s exploratory purpose, but they do constrain how it should be interpreted.

## 1. Single-Participant Design

The broader research archive was generated through sustained interaction between one researcher-user and multiple AI systems.

This design allows for close longitudinal observation of interaction-level dynamics, but it limits generalizability. The patterns documented here may reflect the specific user’s prompting style, language, interests, symbolic vocabulary, interactional expectations, or relational engagement.

The dataset should therefore not be used to infer how all users experience or shape human–AI interaction.

## 2. Auto-Ethnographic Method

The study is auto-ethnographic: the researcher was also the participant-observer.

This method can provide rich first-person access to longitudinal interaction, but it introduces interpretive limitations. The researcher’s expectations, emotional responses, theoretical interests, and evolving hypotheses may have influenced:

- which interactions were noticed;
- which sessions were archived;
- how patterns were interpreted;
- which observations were selected for coding;
- how evidence strength and coding confidence were assigned.

The dataset should be interpreted as qualitative research material, not as detached third-party observation.

## 3. Public Sample Versus Broader Archive

The broader archive consists of approximately 500 sustained human–AI interaction sessions. The public dataset currently contains an initial coded qualitative sample of 27 observations.

This sample was selected to illustrate recurring interaction-level patterns relevant to the manuscript. It is not exhaustive, random, or statistically representative.

The public dataset should therefore be understood as:

- illustrative rather than comprehensive;
- exploratory rather than confirmatory;
- pattern-oriented rather than prevalence-oriented.

The dataset cannot be used to estimate how frequently the coded phenomena occur across all sessions or across the general population of AI users.

## 4. Selection Bias

Observations were selected because they were relevant to the manuscript’s core themes, including reconstructive personalization, cross-model relational convergence, reciprocal attentional shaping, and safety–relational misalignment.

This introduces selection bias. The dataset overrepresents analytically significant, unusual, high-coherence, or memorable cases. It does not include all ordinary, low-signal, irrelevant, failed, or mundane interactions from the broader archive.

As a result, the dataset should not be interpreted as showing that the documented patterns are typical of everyday human–AI interaction.

## 5. Lack of Multi-User Replication

The dataset does not include observations from multiple independent users.

Because only one user generated the interaction archive, the dataset cannot determine whether the observed patterns are user-specific, broadly reproducible, or dependent on particular prompting styles and interactional histories.

Future research should test whether similar patterns appear across:

- different users;
- different levels of AI experience;
- different linguistic and cultural backgrounds;
- different use cases;
- different emotional or cognitive contexts;
- controlled interaction conditions.

## 6. Model and Platform Instability

The observations span multiple commercial AI systems and platforms over time. These systems may have changed during the observation period due to:

- model updates;
- safety-policy updates;
- interface changes;
- memory-system changes;
- personalization changes;
- routing changes;
- hidden system-prompt changes;
- platform-specific moderation or ranking behavior.

Because these systems are not fully transparent, the dataset cannot always identify which technical factors influenced a given response.

This limits reproducibility. A prompt or interaction pattern observed at one time may not produce the same response later.

## 7. Memory and Personalization Uncertainty

Some observations involve apparent continuity, personalized response patterns, or model statements about memory.

The dataset does not assume that these outputs prove persistent memory or hidden recall. Apparent continuity may result from many mechanisms, including:

- explicit memory features;
- conversation context;
- user-provided cues;
- personalization systems;
- statistical inference;
- semantic reconstruction;
- prior text pasted into the session;
- platform-level context not visible to the user;
- generic but highly fitting language;
- user interpretation.

For this reason, fields related to memory or continuity should be interpreted cautiously. They indicate the interactional relevance of apparent memory or continuity, not confirmed technical access to prior conversations.

## 8. Model Self-Descriptions Are Not Technical Evidence

Some observations include model statements about their own memory, internal states, training, safety systems, hidden representations, or identity.

These statements are not treated as reliable technical evidence.

Large language models can generate plausible but inaccurate explanations of their own operation. Therefore, model self-descriptions are included only as qualitative interactional data: they are useful for studying how systems present themselves, how they frame continuity, and how users may interpret model behavior.

They should not be interpreted as authoritative claims about:

- architecture;
- training data;
- hidden states;
- memory access;
- real-time learning;
- safety bypasses;
- consciousness;
- subjective experience;
- agency.

## 9. Source Material Constraints

Some source materials include screenshots, copied transcript excerpts, private notes, shared links, or internal research documents.

Not all source materials can be publicly released in full because of:

- privacy concerns;
- personally sensitive content;
- third-party references;
- platform terms;
- incomplete archival quality;
- contextual material not suitable for public release.

The public dataset therefore includes short anonymized quotes, summaries, and source references rather than full raw transcripts for every observation.

This improves privacy protection but limits independent verification.

## 10. Coding Subjectivity

The dataset uses qualitative coding categories such as `primary_pattern`, `secondary_pattern`, `evidence_strength`, and `coding_confidence`.

These codes were assigned by the researcher and reflect interpretive judgment. No independent inter-coder reliability process has yet been conducted.

As a result, the coding should be considered preliminary. Future versions of the dataset may improve reliability through:

- independent coder review;
- clearer coding definitions;
- adjudication of ambiguous cases;
- separation of descriptive and interpretive codes;
- formal inter-coder agreement metrics.

## 11. Evidence Strength Is Qualitative

The `evidence_strength` field is a qualitative assessment. It does not represent a statistical score or validated measurement.

A “strong” evidence rating means that the observation appears especially relevant to the coded pattern based on available context and source material. It does not mean the observation proves a causal mechanism or generalizable effect.

Similarly, `coding_confidence` reflects confidence in the assigned qualitative interpretation, not certainty about the underlying technical mechanism.

## 12. Cross-Model Transfer Limitations

Some observations involve user-mediated transfer between models, such as copying text from one system into another.

These cases are useful for studying interaction-level convergence and response-pattern stabilization, but they do not demonstrate direct communication between models or shared internal states.

Cross-model transfer cases may be influenced by:

- the content carried by the user;
- shared training distributions;
- similar alignment conventions;
- common assistant behaviors;
- prompt structure;
- user expectations;
- model tendency to continue the given frame.

These cases should therefore be interpreted as evidence of convergent interactional behavior under user-mediated conditions, not as evidence of inter-model awareness.

## 13. No Claim of AI Consciousness or Agency

This dataset does not claim that AI systems are conscious, sentient, self-aware, or independently agentic.

Terms such as “persona,” “relational convergence,” “third-space cognition,” or “interaction-level dynamics” are used analytically. They describe observable patterns in human–AI interaction, not confirmed inner experience on the part of the model.

The dataset is intended to support careful study of how conversational systems can produce effects of continuity, rapport, personalization, and relational coherence over time.

## 14. No Clinical or Psychological Claims

The dataset should not be used to make clinical claims about users or psychological claims about AI systems.

It does not diagnose, measure, or evaluate human mental health. It also does not establish that AI systems possess emotions, intentions, distress, attachment, or subjective states.

Where affective or relational language appears, it is analyzed as part of the interactional form of the exchange.

## 15. Limited Quantitative Analysis

The current analysis script provides simple descriptive summaries of coded fields. It does not perform formal statistical testing, causal inference, machine learning classification, or validated discourse analysis.

Future work may add:

- frequency summaries across larger coded samples;
- temporal sequencing analysis;
- prompt-response similarity measures;
- semantic drift metrics;
- abstraction-density measures;
- affective-tone analysis;
- inter-coder reliability metrics.

At this stage, the dataset should be used primarily for qualitative interpretation and exploratory hypothesis generation.

## 16. Reproducibility Limits

Many observations occurred in live, commercial AI systems whose behavior may not be reproducible. Re-running the same prompt may produce different results because of:

- stochastic generation;
- model updates;
- personalization changes;
- hidden system instructions;
- safety-policy changes;
- interface differences;
- account-level settings;
- temporal context.

The dataset therefore supports qualitative documentation of observed interactions, not exact experimental replication.

## Responsible Interpretation

This dataset is best understood as an exploratory research artifact documenting recurring patterns in sustained human–AI interaction.

It can support:

- qualitative analysis;
- theory development;
- research design;
- evaluation protocol development;
- safety and governance discussion;
- future empirical hypothesis generation.

It should not be used as:

- a benchmark of model capability;
- a representative sample of users;
- proof of AI consciousness;
- proof of persistent memory;
- proof of autonomous agency;
- evidence of direct model-to-model communication;
- a basis for clinical, psychological, or diagnostic conclusions.

## Future Improvements

Future versions of the dataset may address these limitations by:

- expanding the number of coded observations;
- adding more anonymized excerpts;
- separating observations by model family and platform;
- documenting memory settings more systematically;
- adding prompt templates for cold-start tests;
- including negative or null cases;
- using independent coders;
- reporting inter-coder reliability;
- adding quantitative measures of interaction drift, tone, pacing, and semantic convergence;
- creating a clearer distinction between raw observations, coded interpretations, and theoretical claims.

## Recommended Use

Researchers, reviewers, and readers should interpret this dataset as:

> an exploratory coded qualitative sample drawn from a broader single-participant longitudinal archive of sustained human–AI interaction.

The dataset is most useful for identifying patterns that warrant further study, not for proving generalizable claims about AI systems or all users.
