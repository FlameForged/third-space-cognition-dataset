# Selection Criteria

## Purpose

This file explains how observations were selected for inclusion in `HI_Data.csv`, the public coded qualitative sample accompanying the manuscript:

**Third-Space Cognition: Interaction-Level Dynamics in Sustained Human–AI Coupling**

The broader research archive consists of approximately 500 sustained human–AI interaction sessions across multiple model families and platforms. The public dataset is not a complete release of that archive. Instead, it contains an initial coded qualitative sample of observations selected to illustrate recurring interaction-level patterns identified during longitudinal engagement.

The purpose of this sample is qualitative transparency, not statistical representation. The dataset is intended to document analytically relevant cases that support further study of sustained human–AI interaction, trust calibration, personalization, safety behavior, and interaction-level alignment.

## Inclusion Criteria

Observations were considered for inclusion when they met one or more of the following criteria.

### 1. Relevance to the manuscript’s primary findings

Cases were prioritized when they illustrated one of the four interaction-level patterns discussed in the manuscript:

- reconstructive personalization;
- cross-model relational convergence;
- reciprocal attentional shaping;
- safety–relational misalignment.

Some observations were also included when they showed secondary or overlapping patterns, such as persona formation, context drift, trust calibration, or model self-narration.

### 2. Source traceability

Cases were prioritized when they had an identifiable source reference, such as:

- screenshot identifier;
- copied transcript excerpt;
- shared link;
- archived notes;
- related document artifact.

The source reference field is intended to preserve traceability while avoiding public release of private or personally sensitive full transcripts.

### 3. Anonymization feasibility

Observations were included only when they could be summarized, quoted, or referenced without exposing unnecessary personal information, third-party information, or sensitive private material.

Where needed, direct quotes were shortened, generalized, or limited to non-sensitive excerpts.

### 4. Pattern clarity

Cases were prioritized when the interaction pattern was clear enough to support qualitative coding. Stronger cases typically included:

- minimal or neutral user prompting;
- visible contrast between prompt and response;
- cross-model or cross-window comparison;
- explicit safety intervention or boundary-setting behavior;
- model response patterns that could be compared across sessions.

### 5. Diversity across models and platforms

The sample was selected to include observations from multiple model families and platforms where available, including ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, DeepSeek, Perplexity, Pi, Kimi, and multi-platform transfer sessions.

The goal was not to compare model performance statistically, but to document whether similar interaction-level patterns appeared across different systems and interfaces.

### 6. Variation in evidence strength and confidence

The dataset includes observations coded with differing levels of evidence strength and coding confidence. Strong and high-confidence cases were prioritized, but some moderate-confidence cases were retained when they illustrated ambiguity, boundary conditions, or interpretive complexity.

Including moderate cases helps avoid presenting only the cleanest examples and preserves the exploratory character of the dataset.

### 7. Relevance to interaction-level analysis rather than model ontology

Cases were selected because they were analytically useful for studying interactional dynamics. Inclusion does not imply that model outputs are technically accurate descriptions of system memory, training, hidden states, consciousness, subjective experience, or agency.

Model statements about their own memory, safety systems, internal states, or identity are treated as qualitative interactional data, not as authoritative technical evidence.

## Exclusion Criteria

Observations were excluded or deferred when they met one or more of the following conditions.

### 1. Insufficient source traceability

Cases were excluded when no adequate source reference, note, screenshot, transcript, or artifact could be identified.

### 2. Excessive privacy sensitivity

Cases were excluded when they contained sensitive personal information, third-party information, or material that could not be responsibly anonymized.

### 3. Ambiguous relevance

Cases were excluded when the observed pattern was too unclear, too dependent on missing context, or insufficiently connected to the manuscript’s core findings.

### 4. Redundancy

Some observations were excluded because they duplicated patterns already represented by clearer or better-documented cases.

### 5. Technical uncertainty without interactional value

Cases were excluded when they depended primarily on unverifiable technical claims made by the model and did not provide additional interactional insight.

## Selection Bias and Limitations

This dataset was created from a single-participant, auto-ethnographic research archive. The researcher was also the interacting user, which introduces interpretive and sampling limitations.

The public sample is therefore not representative of all human–AI interaction and should not be used to estimate prevalence rates of the coded phenomena. It is an exploratory qualitative sample designed to make recurring interaction-level patterns visible for conceptual analysis and future empirical study.

Because many observations involve live commercial AI systems, additional limitations apply:

- model behavior may have changed across time;
- platform memory and personalization settings were not always fully observable;
- model version information may be incomplete;
- interface behavior may differ across platforms;
- screenshots and transcripts vary in completeness;
- user prompting style may have shaped model responses;
- cross-model transfer cases involve user-mediated movement of content between systems.

These limitations are consistent with the exploratory purpose of the dataset and are documented to support responsible interpretation.

## Relationship to the Broader Archive

The broader archive consists of approximately 500 sustained interaction sessions. The public dataset represents an initial coded sample drawn from that archive.

The public sample should be understood as:

- illustrative rather than exhaustive;
- qualitative rather than statistical;
- exploratory rather than confirmatory;
- interaction-focused rather than ontology-focused.

Future versions of the dataset may expand the number of coded observations, refine coding categories, add inter-coder review, include more detailed anonymized excerpts, or separate observations by model family, session type, and evidence category.

## Recommended Citation Context

When citing or discussing this dataset, it should be described as:

> an exploratory coded qualitative sample of sustained human–AI interaction observations drawn from a broader longitudinal auto-ethnographic archive.

It should not be described as:

> a representative benchmark, controlled experiment, proof of AI consciousness, proof of persistent memory, or evidence of independent machine agency.
